Households in the rebuilt population (3,405 connected MMP households, Mexico interviews 2010–19) by number of links, coloured by the closest tie: core (head, spouse or child in the US), close kin (parents, siblings, other roster relatives) or distant kin only (aggregated link).

## A2_old_vs_rebuilt.png: The rebuild removes the long tail of distant relatives

Cumulative share of links held by households up to a given network size. Old population: 1,019 households resampled with replacement until 10,000 migrants, every counted cousin, nephew and in-law a separate link (54% of links in households with 20+ links). Rebuilt: every connected household once, distant kin collapsed into one link per household.

## A3_close_vs_distant_kin.png: Receipt rises with close kin, not with the number of distant relatives

Share of MMP households (Mexico, 2010–19) that receive US remittances, with 95% Wilson intervals. Left: households without core migrants, by number of close kin in the US. Right: households whose only ties are distant relatives (uncles/aunts, cousins, nieces/nephews, in-laws), by how many are in the US. This is why each close relative is a separate link and distant relatives form one aggregated link.

## A4_occupations_by_link.png: Kin occupations mirror core occupations because they are imputed

Occupation of linked migrants by link type (last US occupation for roster migrants). Questionnaire-only links (most close kin, all distant links) copy a whole profile from a roster donor, so their occupational mix is borrowed. Differences in occupational exposure across households are therefore partly imputation noise; correlated job loss is treated as a sensitivity dimension only.

## B1_cps_employment.png: COVID hit migrant employment hard and unevenly across occupations

Monthly employment rate (employed / employed + unemployed) by occupation of current or last job, CPS, ages 18–64, Mexican origin (fallback: extract has no BPL). Raw monthly rates, smoothed (3-month centred) only where cells are small (some agriculture months). Agriculture is seasonal in every year. These rates drive migrants' monthly ability to send.

## B2_amounts.png: Each migrant sends a fixed routine amount drawn from a lognormal

Gray: MMP self-reported average monthly remittances on the last US trip (heads and other migrants, trips 2000–19, deflated to 2019 USD, top 1% trimmed). Blue: the model's routine-amount distribution, with its spread (σ) from MMP within 5-year periods and its level set to the Banxico 2019 mean per transaction. MMP reports sit higher than Banxico (recall, proxy reports, trip-start deflation); ENIGH averages over months and under-reports.

## C1_calibration_ladder.png: The 2019 baseline matches network types, link types and survey periods

Baseline receipt by group of households: MMP observed share with 95% Wilson interval (black) and model expected monthly receipt probability under 2019 employment (blue), κ = 0. Only the three network-type rows are targeted (α_core, α_kin); all others are untargeted checks. The model has no household-composition terms, so composition differences (older residents, children) are not reproduced. Faded rows: fewer than 30 households.

## C2_receipt_by_size.png: Receipt rises with network size as in the data; core networks hold the remaining misfits

Receipt by network size and type: MMP share with 95% Wilson interval (black) and model (blue), κ = 0. Kin-only households match within about one point at every size. Core networks show the remaining misfits (core + kin with 2 links −12 pp, with 5–9 links +11 pp). Faded points: fewer than 30 households. Old population for comparison (first E0 run, distant relatives as individual links): kin-only 10–19 links 8.1% model vs 8.7% MMP, 20+ links 18.5% vs 8.8%; core + kin 20+ links 77% vs 56%.

## C3_errors.png: Largest baseline errors: household composition and some core-network sizes

Model minus MMP receipt (percentage points) for every group with at least 30 households, κ = 0, sorted. Targeted rows (network type) are within ±1 pp by construction.

## C4_kappa_2019.png: The 2019 data barely constrain κ; the COVID period must

κ: kin stepping-in when the household's core migrants cannot send. For each κ, α_core and α_kin are refitted to 2019 receipt by network type (α panels on the same ±0.5 logit window). Fit error changes by less than half a percentage point across κ = 0–8, and the fitted α barely move, because few households lose all core senders in a normal year. Top right: kin transfers in households whose core migrants cannot send (stochastic 2019 runs, 5 seeds, out of ~1,100 transfers a month).

## C5_household_amounts.png: Household amounts are in a plausible range

Model 2019 baseline, κ = 0, six-month window (stochastic runs, 5 seeds), against ENIGH 2018 recipient households (monthly USD from quarterly MXN). ENIGH captures only ~8% of the Banxico total, so model amounts above ENIGH are expected.
